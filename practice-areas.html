What We Do

Practice Areas

Strive Lex advises businesses and individuals across seven core areas of practice — from corporate advisory and contracts to insolvency, litigation, arbitration, regulatory compliance, and general commercial and civil matters.

Practice Area 1

Corporate and Commercial Advisory

We advise businesses at every stage of their lifecycle — from formation to funding to eventual exit — on the corporate and governance decisions that shape how they operate, with an emphasis on commercial substance over legal formality.

Founders and boards typically bring us in at inflection points: choosing the right entity and cap table structure before incorporation, setting up governance that will hold up once outside investors or a growing team are involved, working through a shareholder or joint venture disagreement before it hardens into a dispute, or preparing a business for diligence ahead of a fundraise, acquisition, or exit. We work directly with founders, boards, and in-house teams rather than through layers of delegation, so decisions get made without unnecessary back-and-forth.

Practice Area 2

Contracts and Business Transactions

Contracts are where commercial intent meets legal risk. We draft and negotiate agreements that protect our clients' interests clearly, without unnecessary delay to the underlying transaction.

Most disputes we see later trace back to a contract that was signed too quickly, or a verbal understanding that was never reduced to writing at all. We review and negotiate agreements with an eye on what happens if things go wrong — termination rights, liability caps, dispute resolution clauses — not just the commercial terms that got the deal done. That includes vetting the enforceability of NDAs before they're relied on, and flagging when a "gentleman's agreement" needs to become a signed one.

Related reading: When is an NDA actually enforceable, Can a verbal agreement be enforced, and Contract clauses worth a second look.

Practice Area 3

Insolvency and Bankruptcy / NCLT Matters

We represent creditors, debtors, and other stakeholders through the insolvency resolution process, and appear before the National Company Law Tribunal and NCLAT on restructuring and company law matters.

Whether you're a creditor deciding whether filing under Section 7 or Section 9 is worthwhile, a director trying to understand personal exposure once a company enters CIRP, or a promoter working out a restructuring before matters reach that point, the practical questions tend to be the same: how long will this take, what does it cost, and what actually happens at each stage. We work through those questions plainly before any filing is made.

Practice Area 4

Litigation and Dispute Resolution

Not every dispute can be resolved through negotiation. When a matter goes to court, we prepare it thoroughly and represent it directly — while always weighing whether a faster, less adversarial route serves the client better.

Commercial disputes rarely announce themselves cleanly — a bounced cheque, an ignored legal notice, or a disagreement over a contract can each be handled through more than one route, and the right one depends on the facts, not a default assumption of "go to court." We also help clients recognise, early on, whether a dispute is genuinely civil, criminal, or both, since the strategy and timeline differ significantly.

Related reading: What happens when a cheque bounces, What ignoring a legal notice actually risks, and Is your business dispute civil or criminal.

Practice Area 5

Arbitration and Mediation

Commercial disputes are increasingly resolved outside the courtroom. We represent parties through arbitration proceedings and mediated settlements, and draft the arbitration clauses that determine how future disputes will be handled.

An arbitration clause that looks standard on paper can determine whether a future dispute is resolved quickly and privately, or drags on for years — the seat, the governing rules, and the language used all matter more than most parties realise at signing. We also advise on when mediation is the more sensible path, particularly where an ongoing commercial relationship is worth preserving.

Practice Area 6

Regulatory and Compliance Advisory

Operating a business in India means operating within a regulatory framework that shifts often. We help clients understand what applies to them, and stay compliant as their business and the rules around it evolve.

This is also where much of our work for overseas firms and NRI clients sits — understanding which Indian regulatory or procedural requirement applies to a specific transaction or dispute, and engaging local counsel or authorities on their behalf where a presence on the ground is needed.

Frequently Asked

Questions Clients Ask Us

Which practice area should I contact you about if I'm not sure?

You don't need to know in advance. Describe your situation through our contact page and it will be reviewed by an attorney who will point you to the right practice area, or confirm that more than one applies.

Do you only take on large or long-running matters?

No. We handle single, well-defined matters as well as ongoing retainers — a one-time contract review or notice reply gets the same attention as a multi-year dispute.

Can you help if a matter spans more than one practice area, such as a contract dispute that also raises insolvency questions?

Yes. Many real matters cut across categories — a supplier dispute may raise both contract and insolvency questions, or a shareholder disagreement may involve both corporate governance and litigation. We scope the matter as a whole rather than forcing it into a single category.

Do you represent both individuals and businesses?

Yes. Our corporate and insolvency work is mostly business-facing, while litigation, arbitration, and general civil matters regularly involve individual clients as well as companies.

Can Strive Lex assist with matters outside Delhi NCR?

Yes, particularly for NCLT/NCLAT matters, arbitration, and advisory work that isn't tied to a specific court's jurisdiction. For litigation before a specific court, we assess on a case-by-case basis, including through local counsel liaison where useful.

How do I know if my matter needs litigation or can be resolved through arbitration or mediation?

It depends on what your contract says, if there is one, and on the nature of the dispute. Many commercial contracts already specify arbitration; where there is no such clause, we assess the fastest and most cost-effective route on the facts, rather than defaulting to litigation.
