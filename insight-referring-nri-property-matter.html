What Overseas Firms Should Know Before Referring an NRI Property Matter

A handful of details, clarified before the referral is made, meaningfully speed up how quickly India-based counsel can actually act.

Category: International Clients Last Reviewed: 3 August 2026
Please note: This article is general educational information, not legal advice on your specific facts. For guidance specific to your business, please book a consultation.

NRI property matters are one of the more common reasons an overseas firm ends up needing India-based counsel — a client's inherited property, a family dispute over jointly-held land, or an encroachment on a property left unattended while the owner lives abroad. A few details, clarified before the referral is made, tend to determine how quickly the matter can actually move once local counsel is engaged.

Confirm the Client's Documentation Status First

The single biggest driver of how quickly an NRI property matter can proceed is whether the client already holds clear title documents, or whether documentation itself is part of the dispute. Before referring the matter, it's worth asking the client directly whether they have the sale deed, mutation records, and any prior registered documents relating to the property — and if not, whether they know where these might be held (a family member, a local revenue office, a prior advocate).

Clarify Whether a Power of Attorney Already Exists

If the client has already executed a Power of Attorney authorising someone in India to act on their behalf, confirming its scope and whether it's still valid saves real time. If one doesn't exist yet, this is usually one of the first things India-based counsel will need to arrange — and it typically requires notarisation and, depending on the client's country of residence, apostille or consular attestation before it's usable in India, which itself takes time worth planning for early.

Establish Whether This Is Contentious or Administrative

An NRI property matter that's purely administrative — updating records, formalising an inheritance, registering a transfer with no dispute — moves very differently from one that's already contentious, involving a family disagreement or a third-party encroachment. Flagging which category the matter falls into (or that it's genuinely unclear yet) helps India-based counsel scope the engagement correctly from the outset, rather than discovering the true nature of the dispute several steps in.

Manage the Client's Expectations on Timeline

Property matters in India, particularly contentious ones, can move at a different pace than clients based abroad may expect, especially where court involvement is required. Setting a realistic expectation with the client before the referral — that this is likely to be a matter of months, not weeks, if litigation is involved — avoids a difficult conversation later that reflects poorly on the referral rather than on the matter's genuine complexity.

A well-prepared referral, even a brief one covering these points, allows India-based counsel to begin substantively rather than spending the first engagement purely gathering information the referring firm could have clarified with the client directly.
